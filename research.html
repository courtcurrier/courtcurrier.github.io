COURTNEY M. CURRIER

how do grassy ecosystems adapt to a changing world?

Deserts, grasslands, and savannas cover nearly half of Earth’s land surface and account for one-third of global carbon fixation. Semi-arid regions alone drive over one-third of variability in the global carbon cycle, owing to their vast extent and sensitivity to global change. I am an ecologist investigating how novel climate and disturbance regimes alter dryland ecosystem structure and function and feed back to global carbon and nitrogen cycles. I combine long-term rainfall, fire, and herbivory experiments with biogeochemical and isotopic tools to uncover mechanisms that govern carbon and nitrogen dynamics. My work primarily focuses on grass-dominated ecosystems, where climate and disturbance strongly influence variability in the global carbon cycle, but I also extend these questions across an herbaceous–woody gradient.

Three main questions drive my research:
1. What controls organic matter inputs to ecosystems?
2. What regulates carbon and nitrogen transformations?
3. What is the storage and persistence of carbon and nitrogen in soils?

Current Projects

Disturbance x climate controls on soil carbon

We investigate how fire frequency, grazing intensity, and precipitation shape soil carbon storage and long-term persistence in grassland ecosystems.

Acclimation of biogeochemical cycles to global change

This project studies how nitrogen and carbon cycles adjust under novel climate and disturbance regimes.

Mechanistic microbial feedbacks to C and N cycling

Examining how microbial biomass, community composition, and enzymatic activity respond to fire and grazing.

Grassland resilience across aridity gradients

We measure ecosystem recovery, plant composition, and productivity along gradients of dryness.
